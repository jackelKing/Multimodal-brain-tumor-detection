# Clinical Practice Guidelines: Normal Brain Neuroimaging and Differential Non-Neoplastic Findings

## 1. Normal Intracranial Neuroanatomy
A diagnostic magnetic resonance imaging scan of a healthy brain demonstrates precise structural symmetry, preserved cortical architecture, and physiological CSF dynamics:
- **Cerebral Hemispheres**: Symmetrical bilateral frontal, parietal, temporal, and occipital lobes with intact cortical ribbon thickness (2.5 - 4.5 mm) and clear demarcation between cortical gray matter and underlying subcortical white matter.
- **Ventricular System**: Symmetrical lateral ventricles, slender slit-like third ventricle, and patent fourth ventricle without hydrocephalic dilatation, transependymal CSF seepage, or midline shift.
- **Extra-Axial Spaces**: Bilateral Sylvian fissures and cortical sulcal spaces appropriate for patient chronological age, without effacement, compressive flattening, or subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- **Posterior Fossa**: Symmetrical cerebellar hemispheres, intact vermis, well-visualized brainstem (midbrain, pons, medulla oblongata), patent prepontine and cisterna magna spaces, and cerebellar tonsils situated above the foramen magnum.
- **Physiological Contrast Enhancement**: Limited strictly to structures lacking a blood-brain barrier: pituitary gland and infundibular stalk, pineal gland, choroid plexus within ventricles, cavernous sinus, and dural venous sinuses. Any focal or nodular parenchymal enhancement indicates pathology.

## 2. Differential Diagnosis: Pseudotumoral and Non-Neoplastic Mimics
When an intracranial lesion is suspected, the following non-neoplastic conditions must be rigorously ruled out:
- **Cerebral Abscess**: Early cerebritis evolves into a mature capsule with smooth, thin, uniform ring enhancement and profound restricted diffusion (bright DWI, dark ADC) in the central necrotic cavity (in contrast to glioblastoma, which has irregular peripheral enhancement and non-restricted necrotic fluid).
- **Subacute Cerebral Infarction**: Cortical-subcortical wedged distribution strictly conforming to arterial vascular territory (e.g., MCA), with gyral contrast enhancement peaking at 2-3 weeks and resolving by 6-8 weeks (luxury perfusion).
- **Tumefactive Demyelinating Lesion (TDL / Multiple Sclerosis)**: Large (> 2 cm) demyelinating plaque featuring an open or incomplete "horseshoe" ring of enhancement oriented toward the cortical gray matter, with minimal surrounding vasogenic edema or mass effect relative to lesion size.
- **Radiation Necrosis**: Delayed focal brain tissue necrosis occurring 6-24 months following stereotactic radiosurgery or EBRT. MRS shows absent choline and elevated lipid/lactate, with low rCBV on perfusion MRI (< 0.6), differentiating it from true tumor recurrence.
