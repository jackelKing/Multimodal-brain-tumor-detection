# Clinical Practice Guidelines: Multimodal Neuro-Oncology Protocols and Multidisciplinary Decision Support

## 1. Principles of Multimodal Tumor Detection and Assessment
Accurate neuro-oncological management requires the synthesis of orthogonal diagnostic modalities:
1. **Grid-Level Convolutional Neuroimaging (CNN)**: Extracts local receptive field textures, edge gradients, contrast differentials, ring-enhancement morphologies, and sub-voxel signal variations across T1, T2, FLAIR, and contrast-enhanced MRI sequences.
2. **Topological Graph Connectivity (GNN)**: Captures spatial tissue architecture, superpixel region adjacency, tumor-to-parenchyma transitional boundaries, microenvironment heterogeneity, and perilesional vasogenic edema percolation networks.
3. **Clinical and Molecular Biomarkers**: Incorporates patient age, neurological symptom presentation (new-onset seizures, progressive focal motor/sensory deficits, cranial neuropathies, signs of intracranial hypertension like papilledema/morning emesis), Karnofsky Performance Scale (KPS), and molecular markers (IDH1, MGMT, 1p/19q).
4. **Multimodal Fusion Engine**: Integrates visual spatial embeddings, topological connectivity graphs, and clinical priors to output calibrated tumor presence detection, 4-class histological subtype classification, and WHO histological grade prognostic risk.

## 2. Integrated Clinical Decision Pathways
- **Pathway A: Suspected High-Grade Glioma / Glioblastoma**:
  - Urgent neurosurgical consultation within 24-48 hours.
  - Initiation of Dexamethasone (4-8 mg IV/PO BID) with PPI gastroprotection for mass effect and vasogenic edema.
  - Preoperative structural and functional planning: fMRI (eloquent cortex mapping for language/motor tracts) and DTI tractography (corticospinal tract visualization).
  - Intraoperative 5-ALA fluorescence-guided resection aiming for supratotal/gross total resection without functional sacrifice.
  - Mandatory molecular profiling: IDH1/2 mutation sequencing, MGMT promoter methylation PCR/pyrosequencing, 1p/19q FISH/MLPA.
  - Postoperative baseline MRI within 24-72 hours to assess extent of resection (EOR) and rule out postoperative complications.
  - Adjuvant Stupp protocol (RT 60 Gy + TMZ) commencing 3-5 weeks post-craniotomy.

- **Pathway B: Suspected Meningioma**:
  - Symptom review: headache, anosmia (olfactory groove), visual acuity loss (tuberculum sellae/sphenoid wing), Jacksonian seizures (parasagittal/convexity).
  - Assessment of bony involvement (skull base hyperostosis or cranial vault erosion).
  - Surgical resectability analysis according to Simpson classification criteria.
  - Consideration of preoperative embolization for giant or highly vascular skull base meningiomas supplied by external carotid artery branches (middle meningeal artery).
  - Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS) evaluation for incidental or unresectable skull base lesions < 3 cm.

- **Pathway C: Suspected Pituitary Adenoma (PitNET)**:
  - Immediate formal automated Humphrey visual field perimetry (HVFP 24-2 or 30-2) to evaluate bitemporal hemianopic deficits.
  - Comprehensive baseline endocrinological evaluation: 8:00 AM serum cortisol and ACTH, serum prolactin (with serial dilutions to avoid hook effect), free T4 and TSH, IGF-1, LH, FSH, testosterone/estradiol.
  - If Prolactin markedly elevated (> 200 ng/mL) and visual fields intact: Initiate medical therapy with Cabergoline (0.5 mg PO twice weekly) with 4-week hormonal restaging and 12-week repeat MRI.
  - If significant optic chiasm compression with visual field deficits or non-functioning macroadenoma: Urgent Endoscopic Endonasal Transsphenoidal Surgery (EETS).
  - Endocrine follow-up for diabetes insipidus, cortisol deficiency (start stress-dose hydrocortisone if indicated).

- **Pathway D: Non-Neoplastic / Healthy Brain**:
  - Reassurance to patient; symptom-directed outpatient evaluation (e.g., tension headache, migraine management, benign paroxysmal positional vertigo).
  - If transient neurological symptoms were present, proceed with stroke workup (vascular CTA/MRA, carotid ultrasound, echocardiogram, Holter monitor) or seizure workup (routine/video EEG).
