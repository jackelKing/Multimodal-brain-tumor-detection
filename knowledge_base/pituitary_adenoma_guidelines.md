# Clinical Practice Guidelines: Pituitary Neuroendocrine Tumors (PitNET / Pituitary Adenomas)

## 1. Classification and Endocrinology
Pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (formerly designated pituitary adenomas) develop from adenohypophyseal neuroendocrine cells within the sella turcica. Under WHO 2022 / CNS5 guidelines, PitNETs are stratified based on pituitary-specific lineage transcription factors (PIT1, TPIT, SF1) and hormonal expression:
- **Functional (Secretory) Tumors (~65%)**:
  - **Prolactinomas (Lactotroph)**: Most common (~40-50%). Express Pit-1 and prolactin. Elevated serum prolactin (> 200 ng/mL). Symptoms: galactorrhea, amenorrhea, loss of libido, erectile dysfunction, infertility.
  - **Corticotroph Tumors**: ACTH-secreting, driving Cushing's disease (hypercortisolism, moon facies, buffalo hump, proximal myopathy, metabolic syndrome).
  - **Somatotroph Tumors**: GH-secreting, driving Acromegaly in adults or Gigantism in children. Elevated serum IGF-1.
  - **Thyrotroph Tumors**: TSH-secreting, causing secondary hyperthyroidism (rare < 1%).
  - **Gonadotroph Tumors**: Typically clinically silent or mildly elevated FSH/LH alpha-subunits.
- **Non-Functioning Pituitary Adenomas (NFPA, ~35%)**: Hormonally silent; typically present as macroadenomas with mass effect symptoms.
- **Size Classification**:
  - Microadenoma: Diameter < 10 mm.
  - Macroadenoma: Diameter >= 10 mm.
  - Giant Adenoma: Diameter >= 40 mm with extensive suprasellar, retrosellar, or parasellar cavernous extension.

## 2. Neuroimaging and Sellar Anatomy
- **Dedicated Sellar MRI Protocol**: High-resolution coronal and sagittal thin-slice (<= 2-3 mm) T1, T2, and dynamic contrast-enhanced T1-weighted sequences through the sella turcica.
- **Microadenomas**: Show delayed contrast wash-in compared to normal brightly enhancing pituitary gland parenchyma (focal hypo-enhancing defect on early dynamic T1CE phase).
- **Macroadenomas**:
  - Expansion and ballooning of the sella turcica with floor erosion into the sphenoid sinus.
  - Suprasellar extension through the diaphragma sellae creating a characteristic "snowman" or "waist / figure-of-eight" morphology.
  - Upward displacement and compression of the optic chiasm (visual pathway compromise leading to bitemporal hemianopia).
  - Parasellar extension with potential cavernous sinus invasion (classified using Knosp Scale Grade 0-4; Knosp Grade 3 and 4 signify encasement of internal carotid artery).

## 3. Surgical and Medical Management Protocols
- **First-Line Medical Therapy for Prolactinomas**:
  - Dopamine D2 receptor agonists: Cabergoline (0.25 - 1.0 mg twice weekly) is the definitive first-line agent, achieving normoprolactinemia in 85-90% and tumor shrinkage in > 80% of cases. Surgery is reserved for medication intolerance, dopamine-agonist resistance, or acute chiasmal apoplexy.
- **Endoscopic Endonasal Transsphenoidal Surgery (EETS)**:
  - Definitive first-line surgical treatment for all symptomatic non-functioning macroadenomas with optic chiasm compression, Cushing's disease, and GH-secreting acromegaly when cure is achievable.
  - Complications to monitor: transient or permanent diabetes insipidus (posterior pituitary stalk traction), CSF leak, hypopituitarism (hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency, hypogonadism).
- **Radiation Therapy**:
  - Fractionated stereotactic radiotherapy (FSRT) or single-fraction Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS) for unresectable, cavernous sinus residual, or medically refractory progressing tumors. Minimum 2-3 mm margin to optic chiasm required for SRS to avoid radiation-induced optic neuropathy (max 8-10 Gy to optic apparatus).
