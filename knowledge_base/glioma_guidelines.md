# Clinical Practice Guidelines: Diffuse Gliomas and Glioblastoma (WHO CNS5)

## 1. Disease Classification and Pathology
Diffuse gliomas are primary central nervous system (CNS) neoplasms arising from glial progenitor cells. Under the 2021 WHO Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System (WHO CNS5), molecular diagnostics are mandatory:
- **Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant (WHO Grade 2, 3, or 4)**: Characterized by IDH1 (canonical R132H) or IDH2 mutations without 1p/19q codeletion. Grade 4 is assigned if microvascular proliferation, necrosis, or homozygous deletion of CDKN2A/B is present.
- **Oligodendroglioma, IDH-mutant and 1p/19q-codeleted (WHO Grade 2 or 3)**: Retains IDH mutation and whole-arm concurrent deletion of chromosomes 1p and 19q, along with TERT promoter mutations.
- **Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype (WHO Grade 4)**: Diffuse astrocytic tumor lacking IDH mutations, exhibiting either microvascular proliferation, necrosis, TERT promoter mutation, EGFR gene amplification, or +7/-10 chromosome copy number alterations.

## 2. Neuroimaging and MRI Characteristics
- **T1-Weighted Contrast-Enhanced (T1CE)**: Classically presents with thick, irregular, heterogeneous, ring-enhancing peripheral margins surrounding a hypo-intense central core of non-perfused necrosis.
- **T2-Weighted / FLAIR**: Massive hyperintense signal extending beyond the contrast-enhancing boundary, representing infiltrative non-enhancing tumor cells embedded in vasogenic edema (peritumoral infiltration).
- **Diffusion-Weighted Imaging (DWI) and ADC Maps**: High cellularity within the enhancing rim causes restricted water diffusion (hyperintense on DWI, low ADC values < 0.8 x 10^-3 mm^2/s).
- **Perfusion MRI (DSC-PWI)**: Elevated relative cerebral blood volume (rCBV > 1.75 - 2.0) reflecting tumor neo-angiogenesis.
- **MR Spectroscopy (MRS)**: Markedly elevated Choline/NAA ratio (> 2.0), reduced N-acetylaspartate (neuronal loss), and prominent lipid/lactate resonance peaks reflecting anaerobic tissue necrosis.

## 3. Surgical and Medical Treatment Standard of Care
- **Maximal Safe Resection**: The cornerstone of therapy is gross total resection (GTR) or supratotal resection of contrast-enhancing tumor, guided by 5-aminolevulinic acid (5-ALA) fluorescence, intraoperative MRI, or functional neuronavigation.
- **Stupp Protocol (First-Line Standard)**:
  - Phase 1: Concomitant radiotherapy (60 Gy in 30 fractions of 2.0 Gy over 6 weeks) combined with daily oral Temozolomide (TMZ, 75 mg/m^2/day, 7 days/week).
  - Phase 2: Maintenance Temozolomide monotherapy at 150-200 mg/m^2/day for 5 consecutive days every 28 days for 6 cycles.
- **Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields)**: Alternating low-intensity, intermediate-frequency (200 kHz) electric fields delivered via transducer arrays worn on the shaved scalp >= 18 hours/day, improving median overall survival.
- **Second-Line / Recurrence Therapy**: Re-resection, re-irradiation, Lomustine (CCNU), Regorafenib, or Bevacizumab (anti-VEGF) for severe mass effect and radiation necrosis management.

## 4. Molecular Biomarkers and Prognostic Indicators
- **MGMT Promoter Methylation**: Epigenetic silencing of O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase occurs in ~45% of glioblastomas, predicting significantly greater clinical benefit from Temozolomide alkylating chemotherapy.
- **IDH1/2 Mutation**: Associated with younger age (mean ~38 years), secondary glioblastoma transformation, hypermethylation phenotype (G-CIMP), and dramatically longer median survival (> 3-5 years vs 14-16 months for IDH-wildtype).
- **Karnofsky Performance Scale (KPS)**: Baseline functional score >= 70 strongly correlates with completion of standard chemoradiotherapy and improved overall survival.
