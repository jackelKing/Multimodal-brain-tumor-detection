# Clinical Practice Guidelines: Intracranial Meningiomas (WHO CNS5)

## 1. Epidemiology and Histopathology
Meningiomas originate from arachnoid cap cells of the arachnoid villi and represent the most prevalent primary non-glial intracranial neoplasm (~37% of all primary brain tumors). Peak incidence occurs between 50-70 years of age with a 2:1 to 3:1 female preponderance due to progesterone receptor expression.
- **WHO Grade 1 (Benign, ~80-85%)**: Meningothelial, fibrous (fibroblastic), transitional, psammomatous, angiomatous, and microcystic subtypes. Characterized by low cellular proliferation (Ki-67/MIB-1 < 4%), well-circumscribed borders, and lack of brain invasion.
- **WHO Grade 2 (Atypical, ~15-18%)**: Increased mitotic index (>= 4 mitoses per 10 HPF), brain parenchyma invasion, or presence of >= 3 minor criteria (sheeting, macronucleoli, small cell change, high cellularity, spontaneous necrosis). Subtypes: chordoid, clear cell.
- **WHO Grade 3 (Anaplastic / Malignant, ~1-3%)**: High mitotic activity (>= 20 mitoses per 10 HPF) or frank malignant cytology resembling sarcoma or carcinoma. Subtypes: papillary, rhabdoid, or tumors with TERT promoter mutations and/or homozygous deletion of CDKN2A/B.

## 2. Neuroimaging and Radiologic Hallmarks
- **Extra-Axial Signs**: Sharp tumor-brain interface, preserved cortical gray matter displaced inward ("buckling"), CSF cleft sign, displaced subarachnoid pial vessels.
- **T1 and T2 Sequences**: Isodense or mildly hypointense to cortex on T1-weighted images; variable on T2 (fibrous tumors are hypointense; angiomatous and softer tumors are hyperintense). Surrounding parenchymal edema varies directly with cortical venous occlusion and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) secretion.
- **T1CE (Contrast-Enhanced)**: Intense, homogeneous, avid enhancement post-gadolinium.
- **Dural Tail Sign**: Linear tapering enhancement of thickened dura mater extending beyond the mass, seen in 60-72% of meningiomas.
- **Bone Changes**: Adjacent bony remodeling, cortical erosion, or focal hyperostosis (reactive vs tumor infiltration), clearly visualized on CT or high-resolution skull base sequences.

## 3. Surgical Strategy and Resection Grading (Simpson Criteria)
- **Simpson Grade I**: Complete tumor excision with removal of dural attachment and abnormal bone (lowest recurrence rate ~9% at 10 years).
- **Simpson Grade II**: Complete tumor removal with coagulation of dural attachment (~19% 10-year recurrence).
- **Simpson Grade III**: Complete tumor removal without resection or coagulation of dura (~29% 10-year recurrence).
- **Simpson Grade IV**: Subtotal / partial resection (~40% 10-year recurrence).
- **Simpson Grade V**: Simple decompression / biopsy (~100% progression).

## 4. Radiation and Adjuvant Therapy Guidelines
- **Stereotactic Radiosurgery (SRS / Gamma Knife / CyberKnife)**: Preferred primary treatment for skull base meningiomas (cavernous sinus, petroclival, cerebellopontine angle) < 3.0-3.5 cm without acute mass effect, or for postoperative residual Grade 1 lesions. Target dose: 12-16 Gy at 50% isodose line.
- **Fractionated External Beam Radiotherapy (EBRT)**: Standard adjuvant therapy for WHO Grade 2 (54-60 Gy) and Grade 3 (59.4-66 Gy in 1.8-2.0 Gy fractions) meningiomas to prevent aggressive local recurrence.
- **Systemic Therapy**: Limited efficacy; considered for refractory aggressive lesions (Bevacizumab, Sunitinib, or clinical trials evaluating somatostatin receptor agonists like 177Lu-DOTATATE).
